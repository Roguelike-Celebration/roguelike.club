2026 Speakers

Alexandra Leonhart
Roguelike, more like Roguedyke: A queer Reading of Traditional Roguelike Conventions

After a decade working as a collegiate IT gal and professor of sound art and general game design nerdery, Alexandra crossed the globe to start her next adventure as a PhD candidate at Victoria University of Wellington, where she studies ludonarrative design and its intersection with queer theory while dabbling in making quirky gay games. She is a quirky, biohacked roguedyke scholar living with her wife in Wellington, New Zealand, where they enjoy lots of great vegetarian food and pinball.

Andrew
Mazes: Are we lost yet?

Andrew Plotkin's life was forever changed at the age of 8 when he got to try Colossal Cave on a teletype terminal. It was forever changed several times after that, too, but the adventure game bug stuck around.

Bjarke Alexander Larsen
Corrupted Databases - Building an (In)finitely Repeatable Logic Puzzle in a Discord Bot

Bjarke A. Larsen is a technical narrative designer and researcher, with a Ph.D. on storytelling in live service games. He is interested in stories and communities, and how the two intermingle. He also has a deep love of database games and logic puzzles and somehow managed to squeeze all of that into a narrative game about sacred trains.

Chelsea Thompto
Spaces for Contemplation: Procedural Generation as a Tactic for Nonlinear Narrative in Art

Chelsea Thompto is a transdisciplinary artist and educator working across art, trans studies, and technology. Her work is focused on the trans body as a site of political potential and engages digital and traditional media. She received an MFA in 4D Art and an MA in Gender and Women’s Studies both from the University of Wisconsin Madison and is currently an Assistant Professor of Creative Technologies in the School of Visual Arts at Virginia Tech.

Everest Pipkin
Dungeons, digging, and wells - the gamers yearn for the mines. This is a retrospective of video game holes.

Everest Pipkin is a writer, game developer, artist, and educator whose work both in the studio and in the garden follows themes of ecology, tool making, and fantasies of collapse and isolation– as well as the labor, maintenance, devotion, and collective care so often present in those fantasies. g blekkenhorst
it's standing right behind you: interface games as metaleptic convergences

g blekkenhorst is a Canadian artist and experimental narrative designer working across multiple media, but tending towards games, ttrpgs, comics, writing, fabrication, poetry, illustration, etc, etc, and next week they'll be someone else. they teach Games & Prototyping at York University, and have had games featured in Superfest and Toronto Games Week.

Their work deals with concepts of fear, vulnerability, monstrous embodiment, cognitive dissonance, reluctant capitalism, never knowing what’s going on inside the meat or the mind of yourself or anyone else and feeling generally haunted. My name is Jamie Brittain, I started my career as a television writer, co-creating the teen drama 'Skins', and working on shows like Dates, Kiss Me First and Breeders for the best part of 2 decades. Michael Howk
Hack: The "Lost Roguelike"

I’m a full stack developer and former field engineer with a systems-oriented mindset. I started restoHack and protoHack as a way to learn C by restoring early Hack source code, which turned into a deeper effort to reconstruct its history directly from original materials.

My work focuses on getting these early versions running on modern systems while comparing them against known variants to understand how Hack evolved. Along the way, I’ve worked with recovered source from PDP-11 backups and used direct code comparison to piece together parts of the game’s history that aren’t clearly documented elsewhere.

This project has been picked up within the roguelike preservation community and is now used in historical demos and exhibits.

Beyond their day job, they make weird games and digital art, and perform as a livecoder. They are still struggling to regain their position as a top-10 speedrunner in a game about flipping coins.

Paris
Please Hold: Building a Playable Museum Exhibit About 1993 Game Support

Paris Buttfield-Addison makes games and tools in Hobart, Tasmania, as a co-founder of Secret Lab (Night in the Woods) and one of the people behind Yarn Spinner, the free dialogue tool that powers everything from DREDGE to Venba to Night in the Woods. He has degrees in medieval history and computer science, is partway through a law degree for reasons that remain unclear, and presents a space news segment on local radio. Most recently he helped build Hint Line '93, a playable museum exhibit about a fake 1993 game hint line. He's currently working on I Feel Fine, written by Ryan North, and Leonardo's Moon Ship, created by Jim Capobianco (he wrote Ratatouille).

Paul Hembree
Found (Mathematical) Objects: Generative Beats Without AI/ML

Paul Hembree holds a PhD in Composition, supported by Computer Music research, from UC San Diego. He has worked as a hybrid composer-programmer on ethical procedural music generation and machine learning at Amper Music and BandLab, where all training data was created by fairly compensated in-house composers. His indie games, developed with Platypus Games, include Rite of Tephra (2019) and Woolgatherer (2023), featuring microtonal orchestral music in non-standard equal temperaments. He has presented at GDC, GameSoundCon, and Roguelike Celebration, and is currently developing foundobjects.org, an independent project using Euclidean rhythms for ethical beat generation.

slashie
