Ancient Roguelikes: The Pre-History of Roguelike Games

Roguelikes existed before Rogue. How can that be? You see, all inventions are just manifestations of ideas that are already in the room. Rogue was the culmination of a lot of previous ideas in the shape of a digital artefact. This talk takes you on a worldwide journey back in time to ancient forms of procedurally generation, tracing some roots of the games we play nowadays.

Bitwise Black Magic: How to Computer, Better Faster Stronger

There's a famous wtf moment in Quake 3 Arena's source code, involving the use of "evil floating point bit level hacking"... but what is that? Hidden inside computer math are a secret set of tools that can help your game run much faster, if you learn the magic behind all of those magic numbers. This talk will be a theme park ride through some of the most impressive optimization techniques I've found or created myself, so math enthusiasts at any level can learn something new. Whether it's using only 1 bit of save data per collectible in huge, randomly generated maps, or using x&-x to instantly find pixel gaps for things like fluid simulations, the hidden math of computers lets you accomplish much more than you probably think!

Don't Save The @: What Roguelikes Teach Us About Story Structure

As someone who has worked in tv, film - and now AAA gaming, I have long been obsessed with story structures, both as a tool to use in my own work, but also as perhaps the biggest grift in the creative industries today. The Hero With A Thousand Faces, Robert McKee's Story, Dan Harmon's 'story wheel', and perhaps most of all, Blake Snyder's 'Save The Cat' all contain much wisdom, but they have also contributed to the mass homogenisation of storytelling across culture. How many times have you been reading a book, watching a movie, playing a game, and have found that you know precisely what is going to happen next, and after that, and after that...

I would like to look at how the stories we experience in roguelike games resist the predictability of story structure orthodoxy. Permadeath, random level generation, ascii graphics and turn based gameplay could all be viewed as antithetical to a gripping narrative structure, but, as anyone who has played one of these games knows, they can create some of the most immersive and emotional experiences we can have with games. I will argue that roguelikes teach us about the importance of avoiding well travelled paths (and structures) when we tell stories.
